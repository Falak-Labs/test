### ELEVATOR
If you tie a rope to a box and pull it through your ceiling… congrats, you just built an elevator.
But with four people inside, your arms quit before floor two.
So you add a motor, a roof pulley, and a counterweight as heavy as the box plus half a load, so the motor barely works.
Now it glides up ten floors… until the rope snaps.
So you add steel rails, backup cables, and a speed governor: if the box falls too fast, clamps bite the rails and stop it dead.
But on floor five, someone opens the door while the box is still on floor one.
That's why every door gets a lock that only opens when the box is there, a light beam across it, and edges that reopen on touch.
Now everything works… until the power cuts out and you're stuck between floors with eleven strangers.
So you add a backup battery, an emergency phone, and a rescue mode that crawls to the nearest floor and opens the doors.
But before you press the first button — follow, because next we're building a skyscraper.
Now just hide the machine behind two shiny doors, add a panel of buttons… and a little ding.
One shaft, twenty floors, zero stairs.
And congrats — you just built an elevator yourself.
And no, pressing the button ten times won't make it come any faster.
### POOL
If you float a wooden deck on your pool… congrats, you just built a pool that turns into a patio.
But when three friends stand on one corner, it tips, and everyone's swimming in their clothes.
So you build it from stainless steel, add air chambers underneath, and cable every corner to the pool bottom, so it stays level.
Now it's rock solid… until you want to swim, and it won't go down.
So you add pulleys, steel cables, and a piston that runs on plain water, not oil, pulling the floor down to any depth.
But twenty thousand gallons of water have to go somewhere every time it moves.
That's why you leave a thin gap around the edge, so the water just flows underneath — no draining, no pumping, no flooding.
Now everything works… until a pool float drifts under the edge while the floor is rising.
So you add sensors that stop it instantly, locks at the top, and a frame that holds a whole party.
But before you press the button — follow, because next we're building a secret bookshelf door.
Now tile it to match your patio, press a button, and in five minutes your patio sinks into a pool.
One backyard, two spaces, zero compromise.
And congrats — you just built a disappearing pool yourself.
### SKYSCRAPER
If you stack a hundred apartments on top of each other… congrats, you just built a skyscraper.
But whoever lives on floor ninety-nine has to climb ninety-eight flights of stairs.
So you add elevators — but that many shafts would eat half the building.
That's why you add express elevators to sky lobbies, where local ones take over — like a train, then a bus.
Now everyone gets home fast… until a storm hits, and the top sways so much people get seasick.
So you hang a steel ball heavier than a jumbo jet near the top, on cables and shock pistons. The tower leans left, the ball swings right.
But then the ground starts shaking.
So you build a frame that bends instead of snapping, add giant shock absorbers every few floors, and drive the foundations deep into bedrock.
Now it can survive almost anything… until a fire breaks out on floor eighty.
So you add sprinklers everywhere, stairwells pumped with fresh air so smoke stays out, and refuge floors where people wait safely.
But before you cut the ribbon — follow, because next we're building a rocket that lands itself.
Wrap it in glass, light it up.
One city block, a hundred floors, ten thousand people.
And congrats — you just built a skyscraper yourself.
