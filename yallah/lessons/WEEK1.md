# Yallah Say: week 1

The plan (the owner, Nov 21 2026): every day, in each dialect, 3 Shorts and one long lesson of about 5 minutes.
This is the first week: 21 Shorts and 7 long lessons, the same lessons in Moroccan Darija and in Palestinian Arabic.
It is a plan, not recordings: nothing here is voiced yet except lesson 01.

## How a day is built
- One theme a day. Each Short teaches 5 phrases and one hard word, so a day is 15 phrases and 3 hard words.
- Every Short keeps the format the owner locked in with lesson 01: a hook (a question about the hard word), five
  phrases with a second to think before each answer, a quick quiz, the hard word in parts and then whole, the word in a
  sentence close to home with its meaning, the viewer's turn, the end card.
- The long lesson of the day is the longer version of that day's Shorts: the same 15 phrases inside one scene, then
  practice. The evening Short can say that the full lesson is on YouTube.
- Every video ends by sending viewers to the accounts and the YouTube channel (the owner's rule).
- Each lesson must have its own subject and its own hard word. Three near-copies a day is what YouTube refuses to pay
  for ("mass-produced" content), so the themes, the scenes and the sentences change every time.

## The long lesson (about 5:00, 16:9, YouTube only)
| Time | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Opening | The teacher greets in the dialect, names the theme and makes the promise for the end. The three hard words show as "? ? ?". |
| 0:20 to 1:20 | The scene | Adam, Lina and the class act a short scene in the place of the day (home, café, souk) in English, using the day's phrases, with the dialect written below. The teacher stops it twice: "what did she say?" |
| 1:20 to 3:20 | Three rounds | The three Shorts' phrases, slower: the teacher says the phrase in the dialect, time to think, the pupil answers in English, then "repeat after me" while the words light up. |
| 3:20 to 4:10 | The hard words | The day's three hard words: parts, whole word, the sentence close to home. |
| 4:10 to 4:40 | Final quiz | Six quick questions mixed from the day, with the clock ticking. |
| 4:40 to 5:00 | Close | The 15 phrases on one card, tomorrow's theme, and "for more videos follow our accounts and the YouTube channel". |

- Picture: the classroom in a wide shot on the left, the phrase card on the right. Every scene is drawn by the same kit.
- Sound: the long lesson reuses the takes of that day's three Shorts. Only the teacher's linking lines and the scene's
  connecting lines are new.

## What the voices cost (measured on lesson 01)
One Short as recorded today is about 825 characters (ma01 806, pa01 843). Of those:
- about 150 are lines that never change ("And now, today's hard word!", "Now faster!", the closing line, "Bye!");
- about 240 to 280 are spare takes that are almost never used;
- about 205 are the pupils' English lines, said by the same two voices in both dialects;
- about 210 are the teacher's lines for that lesson.

So: record the fixed lines once per dialect, record spare takes only when a take comes out wrong, and let both
dialects share the pupils' English lines. A day (6 Shorts and 2 long lessons) then needs about 4,000 credits instead
of about 10,000, which is about 120,000 a month instead of about 310,000.
